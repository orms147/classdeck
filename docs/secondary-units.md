# Secondary units (Global Success 6–9)

The primary pipeline (grades 3–5) assumes a unit of 3 lessons × 2 periods of 35 minutes built from six numbered activities per lesson. Lower-secondary *Global Success* books (grades 6–9) are shaped differently, and teachers usually receive them as **scanned PDFs** (one picture per page, no text layer). This document specifies both differences.

## Unit shape

A secondary unit has seven lessons; each lesson is taught in one 45-minute period:

| # | lesson | typical content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Getting Started | a listen-and-read conversation that introduces the topic, comprehension (fill in / true-false), vocabulary matching, a quiz |
| 2 | A Closer Look 1 | vocabulary (matching, word–picture, gap-fill from a box) and pronunciation (sounds, listen and number, sentences) |
| 3 | A Closer Look 2 | grammar: a *Remember!* rule box with examples, controlled practice (gap-fill, rewrite, match), a game |
| 4 | Communication | *Everyday English* functional language (model exchanges, pair practice) and a culture/places section with a listening task |
| 5 | Skills 1 | reading (pre-questions, a text, vocabulary matching, comprehension) and speaking (questions, a short presentation) |
| 6 | Skills 2 | listening (pre-questions, gap-fill, true/false) and writing (guiding questions, a paragraph) |
| 7 | Looking Back & Project | review exercises (vocabulary, grammar) and the project; the *Now I can* self-check |

The section headings on the page (GETTING STARTED, A CLOSER LOOK 1, …) are the segmentation anchors; numbered exercise lines inside a section ("2 Read the conversation again …") are the activities. Curriculum config for a secondary grade (`curriculum/gdpt-2018/tieng-anh/lop-9.yaml`) has `period_minutes: 45`, `unit_shape: secondary`, the units with the "This unit includes" items as outcomes (vocabulary topics, pronunciation, grammar, skills, everyday English), and **no closed word list**: the vocabulary-level check is replaced by a check that new words come from the unit's own pages.

## Period structure (45 minutes)

`warm_up` (5) → `presentation` (10–12) → `practice` (15–18) → `production` (8–10) → `wrap_up` (3–5). Each SGK exercise maps to one activity in the phase that matches its purpose: input (conversation, text, *Remember!*) → presentation; **vocabulary and pronunciation exercises** (matching, picture matching, gap fill from a box, sound sorting, listen and repeat) and the other controlled exercises → practice; the free or personal tasks (a prompt such as *What other community helpers can you add?*, pair work, games, quizzes about the pupils' own lives, projects) → production. A lesson with no such task gets a short speaking task written by the model from the lesson's words (AI-added, `generated`); the period still adds up to 45 minutes. The textbook's exercises are kept in their order and wording; the deck adds the interaction (pictures, audio, reveal, timers) and the teacher layer (answer keys, steps, timing). **The textbook is the source of truth.** A teacher's own plan (for example a Word file) may be uploaded as *reference*: its timings and warm-up ideas may be used, but its content is never preferred over the book, and its digital-competence and special-needs sections stay out of the pupil slides.

## Interactive exercise blocks

New block kinds (lesson model) so that the deck can run the textbook's exercises in class rather than show them as text:

| kind | content | deck behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `reading` | `title`, `paragraphs[]` (with `highlight[]` words), optional picture | a readable text slide; tap a highlighted word to see its meaning |
| `match` | `left[]`, `right[]`, `answer: {left_index: right_index}`; optional `left_images[]` / `right_images[]` (a citation of an image span, or null, one per item) | tap a left item then a right item to draw a match; *Check* colours right/wrong; *Show answers*. With pictures on the left (a word-to-picture exercise: the word box is the right column) the left column is a grid of numbered pictures and the words are chips; the item's text is the picture's description (alt text, answer-key label) |
| `gap_fill` | `items[]: {text with ___, answer}`, optional `word_box[]` | each blank is a tap-to-reveal chip; the word box is shown as chips that grey out when used |
| `choice` | `items[]: {stem, options[], answer}` | options as big buttons; tap to choose, then green/red feedback |
| `true_false` | `items[]: {statement, answer: bool}` | T / F buttons with feedback |
| `rule_box` | `title`, `points[]`, `examples[]` | the *Remember!* box as a highlighted card; examples with 🔊 |
| `question_set` | `prompt`, `questions[]` | discussion or quiz questions, revealed one at a time |
| `sound_sort` | `sounds[]` (IPA without slashes, from the page heading `Vowel revision: /æ/, /ɑː/, and /e/` or the instruction), `words[]: {word, sound}`, optional `sentences[]: {text, targets[]: {word, sound}}` | a pronunciation exercise. Words: each word is a big chip with 🔊 (the deck's speech synthesis); tap a chip, then a sound column (or drag it there) to sort; *Check*, *Show answers*. Sentences: each is shown with its target words as chips; tap a chip to mark it with the next sound (each sound has its own colour and mark: underline, circle, tick); *Check* / *Show answers* colour the targets by sound. A sound sort has words, sentences or both. Every `sound` is one of `sounds`; every target occurs in its sentence as a whole word |
| `gallery` | optional `title`, `images[]: {image (a citation of an image span), label, caption, alt}` (at most six), `questions[]` | the page's pictures on one slide with the questions about them (*What can you see in each picture?*); the project's pictures are one gallery, never one slide each. Pictures show with their label (a, b), `alt` is the alt text |
| `grid` | `header[]`, `rows[][]` (an empty cell is for the pupils; a `___` cell is a blank), `blank_rows`, optional `answers[]` (one per blank, teacher key) | a table pupils fill in: the project's survey table, *Find someone who ...*, a listening table. Empty cells are editable fields; *Show answers* fills the blanks |
| `self_check` | `title` (*Now I can ...*), `items[]`, `scale[]` (✓, ✓✓, ✓✓✓) | the *Now I can* list as an interactive self-check: tap ✓, ✓✓ or ✓✓✓ per line; the choice is kept for the session |

A `reading` paragraph may carry its own `image`; a `dialogue` may carry `highlights[]` (the phrases the page highlights, shown as chips). The `activity` block has `type: project` for the project's task: its `instructions` are the page's task line, its `student_steps` the page's numbered steps (shown as numbered cards).

Every exercise kind except `rule_box` also carries `instruction` (the textbook's instruction line, shown as the slide's heading) and an optional `exercise_no`; a `reading` may give a `glossary` for its highlighted words, and a `question_set` may give a sample `answer` per question. Answer fields are validated (indices in range, a choice's answer is among its options, a gap's answers match its blanks and its word box). A deck slide holds at most six items; longer exercises continue on further slides. Every exercise block cites the spans it was taken from (`extracted`); answer keys are teacher-facing and also feed the *Show answers* buttons. All interactions work offline, with keyboard and touch, and reset when the slide is revisited.

## Scanned pages

`ingest` detects a page with no text layer and one picture covering at least 80% of it (`ingest/vision.py: is_scanned_page`). When a model endpoint is configured (`classdeck ingest <file> --endpoint ... [--pages 1-2]`, or any `classdeck run`), the page is rendered at about 150 dpi (at most 1600 px on the long side) and sent as an image to a vision-capable chat model with the `read_page` prompt. The reply is a `PageLayout`: blocks in reading order, each with `kind` (section, heading, instruction, paragraph, list_item, table_row, caption, picture, speaker_line), `text`, a bounding box in page-relative coordinates (0-1), and optionally `speaker` and `column`.

- **Spans.** Blocks become spans `p<page>.s<order>` like a text PDF: section/heading to `heading`, instruction to `instruction` (the exercise line, kept apart from the numbered items inside an exercise, which are `list_item`), list_item to `list_item` (a bare exercise number gets its full stop: "2 Read..." becomes "2. Read..."), a dialogue turn to a `paragraph` "Speaker: text", consecutive table rows to one `table` span (rows joined by newlines, cells by ` | `), caption to `caption`. Boxes are converted to PDF points.
- **Pictures.** Each `picture` block is cropped from a 200-dpi render of the page into `images/<doc_id>/p<page>_img<n>.png` and becomes an `image` span whose `text` is the model's short description of the picture (its alt text). A picture without a usable box is dropped.
- **Cache and cost.** The request contains the page image, so the response cache (`CachingProvider`) is keyed by the image; a second ingest makes no call. The CLI prints tokens and cost per scanned page.
- **Highlights and audio tracks.** A block may report `highlight[]` (phrases printed highlighted: the *highlighted parts* of a conversation, bold words) and an instruction may report `track` (the number next to its audio icon). They become `Span.highlights` and `Span.track`; the drafter uses them for the dialogue chips and for *Play track N* in the teacher notes.
- **Offline or failing.** With the mock provider or no key, the page becomes one `image` span (the whole page). A read that fails (network, timeout, an unparseable reply) is tried three times, with a pause between the tries (5 s, then 10 s), unless the endpoint rejected the key; a page that still fails also becomes one picture. Failures are never silent: every scanned page sends a `ScanReport` (ok or not, with the error), the CLI prints a red line naming the pages that were not read, and the local app adds one warning per page and a summary to the job (*N scanned page(s) were not read ... run it again*). A page that was read stays cached, so running the job again reads only the missing pages.
- **Structured output.** The reply shape is described in the prompt and not sent as the endpoint's `response_format`: with constrained decoding the Meta endpoint garbled the `bbox` key on long replies, while a plain request returned clean JSON. The parser also accepts keys that echo the prompt's wording.
- **Models.** On a Global Success unit page (Tiếng Anh 9, getting-started page), `muse-spark-1.3` read the text correctly but spent 4-8k reasoning-heavy output tokens per page (about $0.02-0.04) and its picture boxes were loose; `openai/gpt-6-luna` on OpenRouter read the same page with correct IPA, tight picture boxes and about 2.5k output tokens (about $0.002). Boxes of either model are approximate (a few percent of the page).
- **Versioning.** `EXTRACTION_VERSION` is unchanged: a scanned page used to yield one picture span that nothing could cite, and text pages are segmented as before. Spans of a scanned document depend on the model that read it, so re-reading with another model can change span ids for the same `doc_id`.

## Drafting a secondary lesson

Code: `planning/secondary.py` (segmentation, exercises, skeleton, the call, validation, assembly), `planning/reference.py` (the teacher's plan), prompt `llm/prompts/draft_secondary.md`, `pipeline.build_secondary_lesson`.

`classdeck run <unit.pdf> --grade 9 --unit 1 --lesson 1` (lesson = 1…7, the textbook lesson; there is no `--period` for secondary units) does:

1. **Ingest** (scanned pages through the vision model) and keep the vision kinds: an exercise instruction line is a span of kind `instruction`, distinct from the numbered items inside an exercise.
2. **Segment by section**: the section headings (GETTING STARTED, A CLOSER LOOK 1, …) cut the unit into its seven lessons (`LOOKING BACK` and `PROJECT` are both lesson 7); inside a lesson, each numbered `instruction` span starts an exercise chunk, an `instruction` span without a number continues the line above (`5. QUIZ What is the place?` + `Do the following quiz.`), a *Remember!* heading starts a rule box, another heading (`Vocabulary`, `Pronunciation`) closes the exercise and titles the next one, and a heading that ends with `?` after the items is a follow-up question that becomes a question set of its own. So does an unnumbered paragraph under a table or numbered items that is a question or starts with a task verb (*What other community helpers can you add?*, *Work in pairs...*); such a line is never dropped. A section heading (`Vocabulary`, `Pronunciation`) stays the heading of the exercises after it until the next heading; the sounds of a heading such as `Vowel revision: /æ/, /ɑː/, and /e/` are kept for the exercises under it. A picture with three or more text spans printed on it (a photo behind a conversation) is left out: its crop would repeat the text.
3. **Skeleton and blueprint** (deterministic): the lesson type (`planning/blueprints.py`, see *Lesson blueprints* below) fixes the minutes of each phase (for a Getting Started lesson warm_up 5, presentation 12, practice 15, production 9, wrap_up 4; a phase with nothing in it gives its minutes to the others, so the lesson always adds up to 45; the model's per-exercise minutes only weigh the exercises inside their phase) and which exercise goes where. Types 1 and 2 follow what the exercise is (input text, conversation → presentation; matching, gap fill, choice, true/false, sound sorting, listen-and-repeat drills and every controlled exercise such as *Rewrite ...* → practice; free speaking/writing, prompt lines, games, quizzes about the pupils' own lives → production). The others follow the book's parts: type 3 puts the first *Remember!* box in the presentation and every further rule box in the practice, right before its exercises (a rule, then its practice, in book order); type 4 makes the conversation the presentation, the pair practice and the culture/listening exercises practice and the last free task production; types 5 and 6 read the part headings (*Reading* / *Listening*: the pre-reading or pre-listening questions and pictures and the text itself are the presentation, the tasks practice; *Speaking* / *Writing*: production); type 7 sends the review exercises to practice and the project to production. The exercises are then ordered presentation, practice, production (a stable sort: the book's order inside each phase). When no exercise lands in production the model writes one short speaking task (`production` in its reply, AI-added) and the period keeps its 45 minutes.
4. **Exercise blocks** (deterministic first; a textbook picture is used inside the slide that needs it: a reading paragraph, a picture match, a gallery; a decorative picture or a lined writing area is dropped, and a page picture that no exercise uses is the picture prompt of the warm-up of a Getting Started or Communication lesson; there are no picture-only slides): a dialogue from `Speaker: text` spans; `match` from table rows; `match` with pictures from an instruction, a word box and two or more numbered pictures (each picture span becomes a left item with its description as text, the box words the right column; the pictures are not separate slides); `sound_sort` from a word list under a pronunciation heading (lines such as `___ pack | ___ park`, the blank and number prefixes stripped; the sounds from the heading or the instruction) and from numbered sentences whose instruction names two or more sounds; `gap_fill` from numbered items with blanks; `choice` from items with A–D options; `true_false` from T/F tables; `rule_box` from *Remember!*; `question_set` from numbered or dashed questions (an unnumbered line under them, *Then tell the class ...*, is its `prompt`); `reading` from a text passage; `choice` also from numbered items whose A-D options are set in a two-column table (*A. while | B. when / C. where | D. who*), from items with `(a / b)` (the stem keeps the sentence with `___` for the pair) and from a table of options over numbered statements (*In which place ...? | Hill | River*); an instruction that says to read a text and then match its highlighted words (*Read the brochure ... Match each highlighted word ...*) becomes a `reading` (the first sentence of the instruction) followed by the `match` (the rest), and the matched words are highlighted in the text with the book's own definitions as the glossary; `gallery` from pictures with their questions and labels (a lone picture beside a follow-up question is that question's gallery); `grid` from any other table (header row, empty cells, a survey table with no rows gets four blank rows; a listening table with `(1) ___` blanks gets an answer per blank); `self_check` from *Now I can*; `rule_box` keeps the lines under its `Example:` heading as its examples, with the grammar heading in its title; a `dialogue` keeps the page's highlighted phrases. The wording is copied exactly.
   **The project** (lesson 7, after the PROJECT banner): the pictures become one `gallery` titled with the project's heading, the task line and the numbered steps become one `activity` of type `project`, then each step's questions or table follow as `question_set` / `grid`, and the *Now I can* list ends the lesson as a `self_check`.
5. **One model call per lesson** (the daily model by default) fills what the page does not say: answer keys (from the lesson's own text), glosses and IPA for the new words, a vocabulary block, a warm-up (no new language; activates the topic), a speaking task when the page has no free task, teacher steps with timing for each exercise, a wrap-up, homework, the summary bullets of lessons 4-7 and the AI-added parts of the lesson type (below). Only lessons 1 and 2 ask for a vocabulary block; the others have no new-word flashcards. For a picture-matching exercise it sees each picture's description and gives a letter per picture; for a sound sort it gives each word's sound (`word_sounds`) or, for sentences, the target words of every sentence with their sounds (`targets`: the words the textbook prints in bold; the vision layer does not report bold). The model may not change the textbook's wording or add exercises the page does not have, except the warm-up, the speaking task, the wrap-up and the parts its lesson type adds (labelled AI-added). For a listening task the answers are suggested from the topic (the script is not on the page) and the teacher note says to check them against the recording; an exercise with a track number on the page starts its teacher steps with *Play track N.* Its reply (`SecondaryDraft`) is validated part by part: every exercise needs its entry and answers that fit its items (one answer per blank and from the word box, a letter per left item used once, an option letter per question, a truth per statement, a sound from the exercise's sounds per word, at least one target per sentence and each target a whole word of its sentence), every vocabulary word must be written in the lesson's text (a word cited at the wrong span is moved to the span that has it), the warm-up, wrap-up and (when asked for) the speaking task need a title, an instruction and at least two teacher and two pupil steps. The call is a plain request (no `response_format`, no schema pasted into the prompt; the reply shape is a short skeleton at the end of the prompt) because constrained decoding on the Meta endpoint wrote stray colons and quotes into keys (`ipa:`), which dropped fields; keys are also reduced to their plain names before validation. A network or endpoint failure is tried three times with pauses (5 s, 10 s) unless the request or key was rejected. A sound sort shows the model the words the page prints in bold (`Span.highlights`) and the exact number of entries each list needs. One re-prompt names the problems; what is still wrong after it falls back part by part: an exercise without answers becomes a plain question list (a `question_set` with its wording and no key), a bad vocabulary word is dropped, a bad warm-up, speaking task or wrap-up comes from a template. Without a model (`mock`) every part uses the fallback and the lesson is still complete and valid. The CLI prints the tokens and the cost of the call.
   **Exact wording.** The text of the lesson is normalised to British spelling first (`planning/spelling.py`: a vision model sometimes reads *speciality* as *specialty*; a short list of American forms such as *color*, *neighbor*, *center* is rewritten), and every vocabulary word of the reply is corrected to the spelling the text has (*specialty* → *speciality*, *color* → *colour*) before it is checked. **Complete vocabulary.** The words that the lesson's vocabulary exercises list (the left column of a word-to-definition matching, the word box of a gap fill or word-picture exercise under a `Vocabulary` heading, the words of a picture matching) are the lesson's key words. The prompt names them, a reply that leaves one out is re-prompted, and one still missing afterwards is added from the page without a gloss. The vocabulary then holds all of them (more than the usual 8 when the exercises list more), and a word that a picture-matching exercise matched with a textbook picture gets that picture on its flashcard.
6. **Reference plan (optional)**: `--reference plan.docx` extracts the matching lesson from a teacher's plan (by its "Lesson N: …" heading) and gives it to the model as *reference only* — useful for timing and warm-up ideas; textbook text always wins; digital-competence and special-needs sections are dropped.
7. Grounding, checks, export as for primary lessons. A block that cites several spans (an exercise and its numbered lines, a conversation) is supported when the spans together contain at least 90% of its words (see [source-grounding.md](source-grounding.md)); the exercise blocks are `extracted`, the warm-up `generated` (AI-added), the vocabulary and the wrap-up `derived`. Secondary curriculum files have no closed word list, so the vocabulary-level check is replaced by `english.words_from_unit` (pupil-facing words of model-written blocks should appear in the unit's pages, warn otherwise); `english.pattern_used` gives no result for a lesson without a `sentence_pattern` block (every secondary lesson), so it does not warn. `english.skill_coverage` and `curriculum.outcome_covered` count the exercise blocks (a conversation is listening and reading, a gap fill reading and writing ...) as well as the activities.

**Units without curriculum data.** `--unit` is a number (`ta9.u1` also works). Grades 6-8, and any unit of grade 9 that the YAML file does not list, have their curriculum unit built from the ingested pages (`curriculum/auto.py`): the title is the first heading that is not a section banner (`UNIT 3 MY NEIGHBOURHOOD` becomes *My neighbourhood*), the outcomes come from the lines of the "This unit includes" box (`Vocabulary | ...`, `Grammar: ...`; a line without a label becomes a topic outcome) and, when the box was not read, one generic outcome covers all seven lessons. Nothing has to be configured.

**Entry points.** `classdeck run` is the one entry point for grades 6-9 (`--lesson 1-7`; `--period` is refused; flashcard pictures are off unless `--pictures` is given). `plan` and `draft` stay primary-only. The local app accepts grade 9 (lesson 1-7, no period) and runs the same path; its sample list has no grade 9 sample.

**Deck.** A conversation longer than one slide is cut into balanced slides (`(1/3)` in the heading, smaller bubbles); a matching exercise with long definitions gets a tighter layout; the summary slide sets long words smaller; the teacher note of an exercise block lists its steps (`About 5 min.` then numbered steps) and the source footer groups paragraphs (`p. 2, ¶7-8`).

## Lesson blueprints (one per textbook lesson type)

Every secondary lesson is built from the blueprint of its type, not from a generic skeleton. A blueprint fixes the phases, which textbook parts go where, what the model may add, and a slide budget (pupil slides, excluding dividers and teacher-only slides). The section heading on the page selects the blueprint; the teacher's lesson number (1–7) must match the section order of the unit, and the app shows the lesson type next to the number (*Lesson 7 · Looking Back & Project*).

| # | type | warm-up (5') | main stages | production | wrap-up | budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Getting Started | topic hook: pictures from the page, 2–3 questions (no new language) | conversation (listen & read, line by line) → new words as flashcards (words in the conversation) → comprehension and vocabulary exercises | the quiz / personal questions on the page | recap words, homework | 14–20 |
| 2 | A Closer Look 1 | review game with last lesson's words | vocabulary: presentation of the new words (pictures where the page has them) → the vocabulary exercises → pronunciation: sound sort, words with 🔊, sentences | "What other … can you add?" or a quick personal-use task | words + sounds recap | 12–18 |
| 3 | A Closer Look 2 | sentence game using the unit topic | grammar: *Remember!* rule box with examples (one slide per rule) → controlled practice in book order → second grammar point the same way | the page's game ("Find someone who …"), else a short personalised speaking task | rule recap with one example each | 12–18 |
| 4 | Communication | situation pictures → "what would you say?" | Everyday English: model exchanges as dialogue with the highlighted phrases as chips → pair practice cards (situations) → culture/places: pictures, question set, listening task (gap-fill/table as an exercise; the teacher plays the book audio) | pair interview from the page | useful phrases recap | 12–18 |
| 5 | Skills 1 | prediction from the pictures/title | reading: pre-reading questions → the text as a reading slide (paragraphs, highlighted words with glossary, 🔊 per paragraph) → vocabulary match → comprehension | speaking: question cards → a presentation frame with sentence starters and a timer | 2–3 key ideas recap | 12–18 |
| 6 | Skills 2 | pictures + "who/what/where" | listening: pre-listening discussion → tasks (gap-fill, T/F; teacher plays the book audio, answers in teacher mode) | writing: guiding questions → a paragraph frame (starter sentence, linking words, word count) → a short model paragraph (AI-added, labelled) | checklist for the paragraph | 10–16 |
| 7 | Looking Back & Project | quick-fire word quiz from the whole unit | review: vocabulary and grammar exercises in book order (each its own slide; long ones split) | project: one slide with the task, steps as numbered cards, the page's tables as a fill-in grid, the pictures as one gallery slide (never one slide per picture); *Now I can* as an interactive self-check (✓ / ✓✓ / ✓✓✓ per line) | unit recap | 12–18 |

How the types are built (code: `planning/blueprints.py` holds the plan as data and the text the prompt shows; `planning/secondary.py` applies it):

- **Skills 1.** The pre-reading questions and pictures are a `gallery` (presentation), the text a `reading` with its highlighted words and glossary, then the match and the comprehension exercises (practice). Production is the page's question cards and its presentation task (with the page's *Example:* as a reading), then the AI-added `speaking_frame` (an activity: 4-6 sentence starters, a one-minute timer, teacher steps).
- **Skills 2.** Production is the page's guiding questions and the writing task (its starter sentence is printed in the task's steps), then the AI-added `writing_frame` (starters, linking words, the word count) and the AI-added `model_paragraph` (a `reading` of 60-90 words whose linking words are highlighted). A frame or paragraph that fails validation after the re-prompt is left out and named in the report.
- **Summary slide.** Type 1 the new words; type 2 the words and the sounds; type 3 each rule with its first example (from the page); type 4 the page's highlighted phrases (else the model's 3-6 phrases); types 5-7 the model's key ideas, paragraph checklist and *I can* recap, with a template checklist for type 6.
- **Budgets** are the pupil-slide targets of the table; the renderer splits long blocks, so the real count is read from the deck (`tools/deck_shots.py`).

Rules for all types: textbook wording is copied exactly; every textbook exercise of the lesson appears once and in book order within its stage; pictures from the page are used inside the slides that need them (flashcards, picture match, gallery), never as stray picture-only slides unless the page has a lone picture with a discussion prompt; audio-dependent tasks say *Play track N* in the teacher notes (the track number is on the page).

## Everything fits on the slide

No slide may overflow its 16:9 stage, in any theme, in pupil or teacher mode, with or without the notes panel. Three layers guarantee it:

1. **Budgets at render time**: per-kind limits decide how many items go on one slide (e.g. choice ≤ 3 items with four options, gap-fill ≤ 5 lines, match ≤ 5 pairs, dialogue ≤ 5 lines, question set ≤ 4) and long blocks are split into "(1/2)" slides; long single texts (a reading passage) are split by paragraph.
2. **Auto-fit in the browser**: after layout, each slide body measures itself; when its content is taller or wider than the safe area it steps the slide's font scale down (to at most 75% of the theme sizes) and re-measures. This catches what budgets miss (long words, long options).
3. **A gate**: `tools/deck_check.py <deck.html>` opens the deck in headless Edge, visits every slide in each of four states (pupil, teacher, teacher + notes, edit mode) and fails when any element of the slide body ends outside the safe area or any text is clipped. It runs in tests on fixture decks and is run on every real lesson before it is shown to a teacher.

How it is built (`export/fit.py`, `deck_template.html`, `tools/deck_check.py`):

- **Estimates, not counts.** Each item's height is estimated from the length of its text (a line holds so many characters at the exercise size; options wrap like a flex row) and the block is cut into the fewest slides that stay under a height budget, then re-cut so that the slides are even (7 items: 3 + 2 + 2). Caps on top of the budget: choice 3 items (4 with three-option items), gap fill 5, match 5 (picture match 6), true/false 5, questions 4, sound-sort 10 words and 4 sentences, dialogue 5 lines, activity 5 steps, phonics 6 words, gallery 6 pictures, word cards 4, grid 7 rows, self-check 6 lines. A rule box, a summary, an activity, a reading, a grid, a self-check and a phonics block carry `(1/2)` when cut; pictures that follow each other share slides. Lower-secondary vocabulary is shown as word cards (about four to a slide, each one tap-to-reveal); primary grades keep one flashcard per slide.
- **Auto-fit.** The script sets `--fit` (1, 0.95 ... 0.75) on a slide whose content leaves its body, clears the pencil bar, the source footer or the badge, or has text that runs out of its box; the structure layer applies it as `zoom` to every child of the body, so a theme needs no code for it. It re-runs on slide entry, resize, notes toggle, edit toggle and after a reveal or an edit.
- **The gate** visits every slide twice per state (as shown, and with everything revealed). Teacher-only slides (notes, source map) scroll by design and are not checked. `tools/deck_shots.py <deck> --slides 3,5 --state notes --reveal --out <dir>` takes settled screenshots and says when a slide number is past the end of the deck.
